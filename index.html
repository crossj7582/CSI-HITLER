AMERICAN INTELLIGENCE TRAINING

HITLER'S NEXT MOVE

Europe Intelligence File 09-39 | September 1939

MISSION: PREDICT THE NEXT CAMPAIGN

YOUR ORDERS

To the Intelligence Analyst

You are preparing a confidential briefing for President Franklin D. Roosevelt and the senior American military leadership.

Germany has invaded Poland. Hitler has already expanded German power, broken international agreements, and taken territory without starting a general European war until now.

Your assignment is to study the available evidence and predict where Hitler will strike next. You must identify likely destinations and explain the reasons behind your prediction.

MISSION RULE: Select two to four possible destinations, choose your reasoning, and lock your report. The historical answer remains hidden until you submit your final prediction.

Historical note: The real Office of Strategic Services (OSS) was established in 1942. This is a fictional intelligence-training scenario using the OSS name and a September 1939 starting point.

FILE 02 — HISTORICAL EVIDENCE

Hitler's Expansion, 1933–1939

1933–1935: Consolidating Power

  • January 1933: Hitler becomes chancellor.
  • March 1933: The Enabling Act helps establish dictatorship.
  • September 1935: The Nuremberg Laws strip Jews of rights and citizenship.
  • 1935: Germany expands its military and introduces conscription.

1936–1939: Territorial Expansion

  • 1936: German troops enter the Rhineland.
  • March 1938: Germany annexes Austria.
  • September 1938: Germany gains the Sudetenland.
  • March 1939: Germany occupies the Czech lands.
  • September 1939: Germany invades Poland.
Pattern to consider: Hitler repeatedly expanded German power, while other European countries often failed to stop him before the next move.
FILE 03 — STRATEGIC INTELLIGENCE

What Could Hitler Do Next?

Evidence A: Military Geography

  • Germany borders several countries in Western and Northern Europe.
  • The North Sea and Atlantic are important for trade and naval operations.
  • Control of coastlines could improve Germany's military position.

Evidence B: The War Has Changed

  • Britain and France have declared war on Germany.
  • Germany faces the challenge of fighting against major European powers.
  • Germany may seek strategic resources, favorable positions, or a way to defeat the Allies.

Your assignment: Predict the next destinations and explain why Hitler might select them.

PHASE 2 — HISTORICAL REVEAL

Intelligence Report Locked

April 9, 1940 — Denmark and Norway

Germany invaded both countries. Denmark surrendered quickly, while Norway resisted for longer. Germany sought to protect its strategic position in Scandinavia, including the route for Swedish iron ore shipped through Narvik during the winter and the use of Norwegian coastal bases.

May 10, 1940 — The Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg, and France

Germany launched a major offensive in Western Europe. German forces attacked through the Low Countries and the Ardennes, helping defeat France and drive British forces toward evacuation from Dunkirk.

June 22, 1941 — The Soviet Union

Germany launched Operation Barbarossa, a massive invasion of the Soviet Union, opening a major eastern front.
