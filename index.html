FILE 02 — HISTORICAL EVIDENCE

Timeline: Hitler's Expansion

1933–1936: Power and Rearmament

  • 1933: Hitler becomes chancellor and begins establishing a dictatorship.
  • 1935: Germany expands its armed forces; the Nuremberg Laws institutionalize antisemitic discrimination.
  • 1936: German troops enter the Rhineland.

1938–1939: Territorial Expansion

  • March 1938: Germany annexes Austria.
  • September 1938: Hitler gains the Sudetenland through the Munich Agreement.
  • March 1939: Germany occupies the Czech lands.
  • September 1939: Germany invades Poland.
ANALYST'S QUESTION: Hitler has repeatedly expanded German power. Which neighboring countries might now be strategically important, and what might he hope to gain?

PHASE 2 — HISTORICAL REVEAL

Report Locked: What Happened Next?

April 9, 1940 — Denmark and Norway

Germany invaded both countries. Denmark surrendered quickly; Norway resisted for longer. Germany wanted to protect its strategic position in Scandinavia, including iron-ore shipments from Sweden and access to Norwegian coastal bases.

May 10, 1940 — Western Europe

Germany attacked the Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg, and France. German forces advanced through the Low Countries and the Ardennes, helping defeat France and drive British forces toward evacuation from Dunkirk.

April 1941 — Yugoslavia and Greece

Germany and its allies invaded Yugoslavia and Greece during the Balkan campaign.

June 22, 1941 — Operation Barbarossa

Germany launched a massive invasion of the Soviet Union. The invasion opened a major eastern front and became a central part of the European war.
